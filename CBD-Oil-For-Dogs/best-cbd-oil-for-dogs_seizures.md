#### **What size CBD oil should I buy?**

Choosing the correct size of CBD oil is dependent on a few factors – (1) your dogs weight/dosage requirements, (2) your budget, and (3) how important dosage precision is for you and your dog. Let me explain.

  1. **Your dogs weight/dosage requirements:** fairly self-explanatory. Most brands will list the recommended CBD concentration by dogs weight. On average, for every 10 pounds your dog weighs, brands will recommend you buy a tincture with approximately 100-200 mg of CBD.
  2. **Your budget:** larger concentrations offer much higher value. Honest Paws Well Oil, for example, offers its smallest 125mg CBD tincture for $0.34 per mg, while its highest 1000mg tincture is $0.10 per mg. Likewise, Lolahemp lowest concentration is a 150mg CBD tincture for $0.20 per mg, while its largest 600mg tincture averages $0.12 per mg CBD.
  3. **Dosage precision importance** : For a dog taking 5 mg of CBD per day, measuring 5 mg from a 150mg tincture will be easier than doing so from a 1000mg tincture.



That said, we recommend purchasing a slightly higher concentration. The vast majority of studies on CBD for dogs used roughly 1mg of CBD per pound of the dogs weight. If your dog weighs 10 pounds, a 30mL tincture with 1000mg CBD will have 33mg of CBD per dropper (1000mg CBD / 30mL = 33mg/mL), and so you will need ~ one third of a dropper. **Apologies for the math!**

#### **CBD oil versus CBD treats for dogs with seizures:**

CBD oils are favored for seizure management, not only due to CBD’s enhanced bioavailability when taken with high-fat foods — a key part for maximizing the therapeutic effects — but also the fact that oils also allow for precise dosing. CBD treats, however, are appreciated for their convenience, and the fact that dogs love treats. #### **Full-Spectrum vs. Broad-Spectrum vs. Isolate CBD for dogs with seizures:**

  1. **Full-spectrum CBD** , containing all cannabinoids including trace THC, is recommended not just for its comprehensive benefits but also because all successful studies on CBD reducing seizures in dogs have utilized full-spectrum oil.
  2. **Broad-spectrum CBD** is THC-free but retains other cannabinoids, offering some of the benefits of full-spectrum. However, trace amounts of THC are very beneficial. 

Removing it not only removes a valuable cannabinoid (again, in very small, safe amounts) but also adds another level of processing to the oil, resulting in a less natural product.

***Note:** full-spectrum CBD oils for dogs must contain less than 0.3% THC, a level that does not produce psychoactive effects. Most full-spectrums include much less, typically between 0.03 and 0.1%.

  3. **Isolate CBD** is pure CBD, which has undergone intense processing to remove all other cannabinoids. Not recommended for dogs with pain, as this is the least effective type of CBD.



The preference for full-spectrum CBD oil is due to its “entourage effect,” where the combined effect of naturally-derived cannabinoids, terpenes, and flavonoids is more effective than CBD alone.

This “entourage effect” synergy is believed to enhance the therapeutic effects in a similar way as consuming whole foods better than taking a vitamin tablet.

For seizures specifically, this aligns with the findings of clinical studies, as all CBD studies that reported a reduction in canine seizures were performed using full-spectrum CBD oil.
